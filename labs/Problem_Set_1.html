Did Neighborhood Bonding Social Capital Differ Between Central and Outer Shanghai?

The question：

Did Neighborhood Bonding Social Capital Differ Between Central and Outer Shanghai?

The data

This analysis uses data from the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown Study, an online survey conducted between April 29 and June 1, 2022. The survey was distributed in Chinese through Wenjuan Xing to adults who were in Shanghai during the lockdown. Participants were recruited through purposive sampling, with a target of approximately 200 respondents in each of Shanghai's 16 districts.

The analysis uses 2,795 respondents with available geographic coordinates. These coordinates allow each respondent to be assigned to a district through a spatial join. The findings therefore describe the respondents included in this analytical sample, rather than representing all residents of Shanghai.

LAB04_shanghai-districts.geojson contains polygons representing Shanghai's sixteen administrative districts. Its attributes include official administrative codes, Chinese and English district names, and a survey_code field used to compare survey records with spatial assignments.

What I found

Central districts scored higher than outer districts, the opposite of what I expected. The mean score was 11.57 in central Shanghai and 11.18 in outer Shanghai, a difference of 0.39 points (Table 1). This difference is statistically significant (Welch t(2,328) = 3.46, p = 0.0006) but small: it is about 3.5% of the outer mean, and Cohen’s d is 0.13. Both regions score slightly below the scale midpoint of 12.

The district-level results show that the two regions overlap a great deal. Outer districts range from 10.26 to 11.90 and central districts from 11.07 to 11.81, so the variation inside each group is larger than the 0.39-point gap between them. The three lowest districts are all outer districts: Fengxian (10.26), Qingpu (10.65) and Jiading (10.89). The highest mean belongs to an outer district, Chongming (11.90), but it rests on only 67 respondents and has the widest confidence interval (±0.85).

The analysis shows a small difference in average neighborhood bonding social capital between central and outer Shanghai.

The mean score for central Shanghai was 11.57, based on 1,081 respondents across seven districts. The mean score for outer Shanghai was 11.18, based on 1,714 respondents across nine districts. The difference between the two regional means was 0.39 points, with central Shanghai scoring higher.

This result is opposite to the expected direction proposed in the presentation. However, the difference is relatively small, representing approximately 3.5% of the outer Shanghai mean.
